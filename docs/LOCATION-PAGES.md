# Location pages — plan, and why none were published

**Status: none published.** The brief asks for individual location pages only
where they contain genuinely useful and unique information. Right now they would
not, so they have not been created. `/service-areas` was built instead: one hub
page with real, differentiated content for each area.

## Why not now

A location page earns its place when it has, for that specific town: real
projects, original photographs, area-specific flooring considerations, and its
own FAQs. Today Floor Candy has **no per-town project attribution** — the job
photos in `assets/img/` are not tagged to a suburb. Publishing eight pages that
differ only by place name is a doorway-page pattern and a demonstrated ranking
liability, not a shortcut to local coverage.

## What unlocks each page

For any location page to ship, that town needs **at least two of**:

1. A completed job in or near it, with photographs Floor Candy owns.
2. A genuine local consideration that changes the work (coastal exposure, slab
   age typical of the area's housing stock, shed/rural work, holiday-home use).
3. A distinct FAQ — something people in that town actually ask.

Plus, for all of them: what Jarrod can honestly say about travel and lead time
to that town.

## The one thing that unblocks most of this

**Ask Jarrod to note the suburb against each job photo.** Once even four or five
photographs are attributed to Forster, Taree, Harrington and Blueys Beach, pages
1–4 become writable with genuine local proof, and the service-areas hub can link
down to them properly.

## Interim measure already live

`/service-areas` gives each primary town its own paragraph describing the work
actually done there and the local factors that change it, plus a separate,
honest section on how travel to Newcastle, Maitland and Sydney is decided. That
captures most of the local relevance without publishing thin pages.
